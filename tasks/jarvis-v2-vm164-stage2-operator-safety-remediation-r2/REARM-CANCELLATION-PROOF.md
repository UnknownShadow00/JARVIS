# Cancellation and distinct rearm proof

The original audit's `test_rearm_a_firing_must_not_be_accepted_as_expected` fails against R1: a successful same-boot callback at deadline+1 microsecond is accepted although rearm-a must be canceled. The untouched failing assertion and traceback are retained in baseline-audit30.log. The R2 replay passes it, and a new fully bound test also rejects this callback.

R2 never routes rearm-a through callback-success acceptance. `outcome` rejects rearm-a, cancel and inactive regardless of callback validity. `binding.cancellation` requires exact source/generation/unit/boot binding, no JSON callback or guard event, no service InvocationID/ExecMainCode or timer LastTrigger, cancellation at least five seconds before the original deadline, and complete journal coverage beginning no later than creation. It requires final inactive/failed pair, zero PIDs and empty cgroup. The original deadline is never moved.

Observe through at least deadline+2 seconds, then retain the canceled generation's tombstone. Rearm-b uses a distinct case name, generation, fresh future deadline and new one-shot creation. It cannot reuse rearm-a or a failed name. Require all rearm-a proofs before admitting rearm-b; later rearm-b success cannot remove a prior failure. At final closure, observe rearm-a again after rearm-b's final evidence, with its original creation-to-closure journal coverage intact. Any early or late callback/guard/invocation for rearm-a fails the cancellation test, even if rearm-b succeeds.

Each rearm-b callback must match its service's actual InvocationID, expected generation-bound guard, original boot, UID/eUID65534, both clocks and expected argv/exit. Old callback bodies cannot satisfy a new generation merely because their payload does not contain a generation: the surrounding raw journal unit/invocation and guard provide that binding. Raw journal JSON-lines are reparsed and compared with the selected events. Duplicate cursors or invocations, missing events, altered file bytes and stale bindings fail.

This proves the acceptance predicate on synthetic evidence. It does not qualify real systemd cancellation, guarantee future inactivity after host failure, or permit a post-expiry action. The human reviewer must establish authentic contiguous journal capture and exclusive administrator custody; hashes cannot establish those facts by themselves.
