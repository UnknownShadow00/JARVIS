One concurrent shadow generation alone does not serialize legacy calls or unload.

| Contract option | Protection / cost | Required decisions |
|---|---|---|
| A: explicit per-host/model shared generation lease and unload arbiter | Coordinates legacy and later shadow at actual remote lifecycle boundaries; precise but touches both resource and generation owners | Owner of admission/counters; exact owned model set; unload refuses/defers/drains; streaming/cancel/disconnect timeout; remote completion/quarantine; release/crash recovery; deadlock/order and legacy behavior |
| B: retain candidate ownership throughout a measured window, plus in-flight protection | Prevents opportunistic unload of candidate even between generations; stronger retention may conflict with sleep/VRAM goals | Claim/release authority and overlap, manual/idle sleep disposition, capacity/cleanup/drain, legacy model sharing, restart/unknown remote activity |
| C: keep current lifecycle and leave live shadow blocked | No legacy interference | Explicitly retain D06/D07/provider activation block |

Recommend an ownership-only contract choosing A versus a narrowly pinned variant of B before production lifecycle changes. No mutex design, new service/process, numeric wait policy, model load/unload or protected model name was invented here. The selected provider identity remains previous authority; the missing part is ownership/coordination, not a new provider selection.
